Questions
How does this help describe a system or single-line diagram?
exceedance supplies a profile's peak kW, energy kWh and interval counts against a caller-stated cap. They could annotate a load at a connection point. This page establishes no actual bus, agreement, import allocation or asset identity.
Why are power and energy separate?
Power is the height; energy is height multiplied by duration and added across intervals. The spike and plateau presets both have 12,000 kW peak excess. At half-hour intervals, the module returns 6,000 kWh and 48,000 kWh respectively: eight times the energy, not an inferred eightfold cost.
What is connected next?
No downstream design is established. A battery would need an explicit state-of-charge and charge/discharge model, controls, efficiencies, limits and an engineering study. This page does not invoke a battery sizing function.
What happens to a zero profile?
Its energy and peak excess are zero. Load factor would divide zero by zero; the source's NaN is explicitly tagged as undefined in JSON and is never shown as a finite percentage.
Machine detail

Pinned source proof · Lab contract

One direct exceedance call for the whole profile, and one for each inspected interval record. Source numeric results are not reimplemented. The page draws rectangles, scales axes, formats values and removes unsupported source narrative. The zero-load-factor special case is tagged, not replaced by zero.

Inputs: 1–48 dense, finite, nonnegative kW readings; positive finite cap and interval hours. Interface policy bounds positive power values to 0.001–10,000,000 kW and intervals to at least one second; the source refuses intervals above 24 hours. Empty cells, negative values, nonfinite values and sparse arrays are refused. Missing readings are not silently zero-filled.

The source's own proof is a verified static caller at the pin. Cross-repository runtime callers are not exhaustively audited. Source proof comments use MW around numeric kW inputs; this lab uses explicit kW inputs and kWh outputs. Passing arithmetic tests do not verify a real profile, connection agreement or engineering design.
